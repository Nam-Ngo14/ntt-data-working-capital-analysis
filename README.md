# NTT DATA Working-Capital Efficiency & Cash-Conversion Analysis

> **Independent financial analysis of NTT DATA's working-capital efficiency, peer positioning, and potential cash-release opportunity.**

This project evaluates how efficiently **NTT DATA converts operating activity into cash** relative to comparable global IT-services companies.

The analysis combines:

**Historical Analysis → Peer Benchmarking → Diagnosis → Financial Opportunity**

The main finding is that NTT DATA's clearest peer-relative working-capital weakness is concentrated in **Trade Receivables and Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)** rather than across the entire operating cycle.

## Research Question

**How efficiently does NTT DATA convert its operations and project activity into cash compared with comparable IT-services peers, where are the main working-capital inefficiencies or advantages, and what could improving those areas be worth financially?**

The project addresses this question in three stages:

1. Evaluate NTT DATA's historical working-capital performance.
2. Compare NTT DATA with relevant global peers.
3. Estimate the financial impact of improving the most important identified weakness.

# 1. Understanding the Operating Cycle

For an IT-services company such as NTT DATA, cash does not necessarily arrive when work is performed or Revenue is recognized.

The operating process can be simplified as:

### Work Performed → Contract Asset → Trade Receivable → Cash

A **Contract Asset** represents work that has been performed but has not yet become an unconditional receivable.

Once billing conditions are satisfied, the balance becomes a **Trade Receivable**.

The receivable remains outstanding until the customer pays.

At the same time, the company receives operating funding through:

* **Trade Payables** — financing provided by suppliers.
* **Contract Liabilities** — customer payments or billings received before the related work has been fully completed.

Therefore, the analysis considers both:

**Capital absorbed**

`Trade Receivables + Contract Assets + Inventory`

and:

**Operating funding**

`Trade Payables + Contract Liabilities`

---

# 2. Methodology

Raw financial data were collected primarily from company annual reports, financial statements, securities filings, earnings materials and investor-relations disclosures.

Where possible, days-based working-capital ratios use average balance-sheet balances.

### Average Balance

**Average Balance = (Opening Balance + Closing Balance) ÷ 2**

### Days Sales Outstanding

**DSO = Average Trade Receivables ÷ Revenue × 365**

### Contract Asset Days

**Contract Asset Days = Average Contract Assets ÷ Revenue × 365**

### Days Inventory Outstanding

**DIO = Average Inventory ÷ Cost of Sales × 365**

### Days Payables Outstanding

**DPO = Average Trade Payables ÷ Cost of Sales × 365**

### Contract Liability Days

**Contract Liability Days = Average Contract Liabilities ÷ Revenue × 365**

### Cash Conversion Cycle

**CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO**

### Adjusted Cash Conversion Cycle

**Adjusted CCC = DSO + Contract Asset Days + DIO − DPO − Contract Liability Days**

This adjusted measure incorporates both **project-stage capital absorption** and **customer funding**, making it particularly relevant for an IT-services business.

### Operating Working Capital

**OWC = Trade Receivables + Contract Assets + Inventory − Trade Payables − Contract Liabilities**

### Standardized EBITDA

**Standardized EBITDA = Operating Profit + Depreciation + Amortization**

If a required financial balance was unavailable or insufficiently comparable, the corresponding metric was treated as **unavailable rather than estimated or assumed to be zero**.

# 3. Historical NTT DATA Diagnosis

NTT DATA's historical data indicate that **Trade Receivables are the main source of working-capital pressure**.

DSO remained relatively stable around **79–80 days between FY2019 and FY2021**, before increasing to:

* **87.3 days in FY2022**
* **98.2 days in FY2023**

FY2022 is treated cautiously because the consolidation of **NTT Ltd.** materially changed NTT DATA's business scope.

By comparison:

* Contract Asset Days remained relatively stable at approximately **11–15 days**.
* Inventory remained a relatively small part of the operating cycle.
* DPO increased, providing greater supplier financing.
* Contract Liabilities remained significant, providing customer funding.

This indicates that the strongest pressure was concentrated in **outstanding Trade Receivables**, rather than unbilled work or inventory.

---

# 4. Peer Benchmarking

NTT DATA was benchmarked against **Accenture, Capgemini, CGI and TCS**.

The largest peer-relative difference appears in DSO:

|                 |            DSO |
| --------------- | -------------: |
| **NTT DATA**    |  **80.1 days** |
| **Peer Median** |  **57.2 days** |
| **Gap**         | **+22.9 days** |

NTT DATA therefore carries materially more Trade Receivables relative to Revenue than the peer benchmark.

However, the broader working-capital picture is more balanced.

### Relative Strengths

**Contract Asset Days**

NTT DATA's Contract Asset Days are below the peer benchmark, suggesting that the company is not comparatively weak in the stage before receivables are recognized.

**Supplier Funding**

NTT DATA's DPO is moderately above the peer benchmark, indicating greater supplier financing.

**Customer Funding**

Contract Liability Days are substantially above the peer benchmark, providing an important source of operating funding.

**Cash Conversion**

CFO relative to standardized EBITDA is broadly in line with peers.

---

# 5. Core Diagnosis

The analysis therefore identifies:

> ### Trade Receivables and DSO as NTT DATA's clearest working-capital opportunity.

The evidence does **not** indicate a broad working-capital problem across the entire operating cycle.

Instead:

* **DSO is materially above peers**
* Contract Asset Days are comparatively favorable
* Supplier funding provides an offset
* Customer funding provides a significant offset
* Operating cash conversion remains broadly around peer levels

The exact cause of the DSO gap cannot be established from public financial statements alone.

Possible structural differences may include:

* customer mix;
* geography;
* contract structure;
* payment terms;
* invoicing processes;
* customer disputes;
* collection effectiveness.

For this reason, the analysis treats the DSO gap as an **observable opportunity**, rather than assuming that the entire difference can be eliminated.

---

# 6. DSO Improvement Scenarios

Full convergence with the peer median would be an aggressive assumption.

Three partial-improvement scenarios were therefore developed:

| Scenario          | Gap Closed | DSO Improvement | Resulting DSO |
| ----------------- | ---------: | --------------: | ------------: |
| Conservative      |        25% |        5.7 days |     74.4 days |
| **Base**          |    **50%** |   **11.4 days** | **68.7 days** |
| Upside            |        75% |       17.2 days |     63.0 days |
| Full Convergence* |       100% |       22.9 days |     57.2 days |

* **Full convergence is included as a theoretical reference only.**

### Selected Base Case

The main scenario assumes that NTT DATA closes:

**50% of the observed DSO gap**

This implies:

**80.1 days → 68.7 days**

or approximately:

**11.4 days of DSO improvement**

The resulting DSO remains above the peer median, meaning the Base case does not assume that NTT DATA immediately becomes identical to its peers.

---

# 7. Financial Opportunity

Normalized FY2025 operating Revenue used in the opportunity analysis:

## **¥4,875.1bn**

Potential cash release is calculated as:

**Cash Release = Revenue × DSO Improvement ÷ 365**

### Scenario Results

| Scenario          | DSO Improvement | Resulting DSO | Potential Cash Release |
| ----------------- | --------------: | ------------: | ---------------------: |
| Conservative      |        5.7 days |     74.4 days |           **~¥76.4bn** |
| **Base**          |   **11.4 days** | **68.7 days** |          **~¥152.7bn** |
| Upside            |       17.2 days |     63.0 days |          **~¥229.1bn** |
| Full Convergence* |       22.9 days |     57.2 days |          **~¥305.5bn** |

* Reference only.

### Base-Case Result

## **~¥152.7bn Potential Cash Release**

This represents capital moving from **Trade Receivables into cash**.

It is therefore important to distinguish the result from earnings.

**¥152.7bn is not:**

* additional Revenue;
* additional EBIT;
* additional EBITDA;
* additional Net Income.

It represents a **potential one-time balance-sheet release** associated with maintaining a lower level of Trade Receivables relative to Revenue.

---

# 8. Potential Financing Benefit

If the released cash is used to reduce or avoid interest-bearing financing, it could also reduce financing costs.

The analysis estimates a historical effective financing-cost proxy of approximately:

## **3.84%**

The financing benefit is calculated as:

**Annual Financing Benefit = Cash Release × Financing Cost**

Under the Base case:

**¥152.7bn × 3.84% ≈ ¥5.9bn**

Therefore:

## **~¥5.9bn Potential Annual Pre-Tax Financing Benefit**

under **100% financing utilization**.

If 75% of the released cash is used to reduce or avoid financing:

## **~¥4.4bn per year**

The financing benefit is conditional on how the released cash is ultimately allocated.

---

# 9. Final Conclusion

NTT DATA does not appear to have a broad working-capital problem.

The clearest peer-relative weakness is concentrated in **Trade Receivables**.

NTT DATA's clean-period DSO is approximately **22.9 days above the peer median**.

Under the selected Base scenario:

> **50% peer-gap closure → 11.4-day DSO improvement → 68.7-day resulting DSO**

At normalized FY2025 operating scale, this could potentially release approximately:

# **¥152.7bn**

from Trade Receivables.

If fully used to reduce or avoid interest-bearing financing, the associated annual pre-tax financing benefit could be approximately:

# **¥5.9bn**

The analysis therefore supports a **targeted focus on Trade Receivable efficiency rather than broad working-capital restructuring**.

# Key Analytical Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the results.

### Peer Differences

The peer companies differ in:

* customer mix;
* geographic exposure;
* contract structure;
* payment terms;
* accounting presentation.

The entire observed DSO gap therefore cannot automatically be considered operationally removable.

### Structural Break

FY2022 is treated as a **structural-break year** because the consolidation of NTT Ltd. materially changed NTT DATA's business scope.

### Scenario Analysis

The **25%, 50% and 75% gap-closure assumptions are analytical scenarios, not forecasts**.

### Financing Cost

The **3.84% financing-cost assumption is a historical effective financing-cost proxy**, not a guaranteed future borrowing rate.

### Capital Allocation

The estimated financing benefit depends on how management ultimately uses any cash released from working capital.
