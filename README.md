Independent working-capital and cash-conversion analysis of NTT DATA, including historical analysis, peer benchmarking, DSO opportunity sizing, and financial impact estimation.


**NTT DATA Working-Capital Efficiency and Cash-Conversion Analysis**

Research Objective

This project evaluates how efficiently NTT DATA converts its operating activity into cash relative to comparable global IT-services companies. The analysis focuses on identifying where capital is tied up within the operating cycle, whether these patterns represent genuine weaknesses or structural characteristics of the business, and what improving the most significant inefficiency could be worth financially.

The core research question is:

How efficiently does NTT DATA convert its operations and project activity into cash compared with comparable IT-services peers, where are the main working-capital inefficiencies or advantages, and what could improving those areas be worth financially?

To answer this question, the analysis combines historical financial data, working-capital ratios, peer benchmarking and scenario-based opportunity sizing.
The analysis follows three steps:

Evaluate NTT DATA’s historical working-capital performance.
Compare NTT DATA with relevant global peers.
Estimate the financial impact of improving the most important identified weakness.
The overall analytical process is therefore:
Historical Analysis -> Peer Benchmarking -> Diagnosis -> Financial Opportunity

Understanding NTT DATA's Operating Cycle
For an IT-services company such as NTT DATA, cash does not necessarily arrive at the same time that work is performed or revenue is recognized.
The operating process can be simplified as:
Work performed -> Contract Asset -> Trade Receivable -> Cash
A contract asset represents work that has already been performed but has not yet become an unconditional receivable. Once billing conditions are satisfied, the balance becomes a trade receivable. The receivable remains outstanding until the customer pays.
At the same time, NTT DATA receives operating funding from suppliers and customers. Trade payables allow the company to delay payments to suppliers, while contract liabilities represent customer payments or billings received before the related work has been fully completed.
The working-capital analysis therefore considers both sides of the operating cycle. Trade receivables, contract assets and inventory can tie up operating capital, while trade payables and contract liabilities provide funding that helps offset this requirement. The analysis therefore examines not only where cash is absorbed, but also where NTT DATA receives operating funding. 


Data and Methodology
The analysis uses NTT DATA financial data from FY2015 to FY2025, together with comparable data from Accenture, Capgemini, CGI and Tata Consultancy Services (TCS). Raw financial figures were collected from company annual reports and financial disclosures before being converted into working-capital and cash-conversion metrics.
Where possible, balance-sheet ratios use average balances:
Average Balance= (Opening Balance + Closing Balance) ÷ 2
The main working-capital measures are calculated as follows:
DSO = (Average Trade Receivables) ÷ (Revenue) x 365
Contract Asset Days = (Average Contract Assets) ÷ (Revenue) x 365 
DIO = (Average Inventory) ÷ (Cost of Sales) x 365 
DPO = (Average Trade Payables) ÷ (Cost of Sales) x 365 
Contract Liability Days = (Average Contract Liabilities​) ÷ (Revenue) x 365 
OWC = Trade Receivables + Contract Assets + Inventory − Trade Payables − Contract Liabilities 
Standardized EBITDA = Operating Profit + Depreciation + Amortization 

CCC=DSO+DIO−DPO 
Because contract assets and contract liabilities are also important in an IT-services business, an adjusted measure is also used:
Adjusted CCC= DSO + Contract Asset Days + DIO − DPO − Contract Liability Days 
This expands the traditional CCC by incorporating Contract Assets, which absorb operating capital, and Contract Liabilities, which provide customer funding. This is particularly relevant for an IT-services and project-based business such as NTT DATA. 
Peer comparability is assessed separately for each metric because accounting disclosures differ across companies. If a required balance is unavailable or not sufficiently comparable, the metric is treated as unavailable rather than estimated or assumed to be zero.
FY2022 is treated as a structural-break year for NTT DATA because the consolidation of NTT Ltd. materially changed the company's scope. The year is retained for historical context but excluded where a clean historical benchmark is required.

Historical NTT DATA Working-Capital Diagnosis
NTT DATA’s historical working-capital data suggest that Trade Receivables are the main source of operating-capital pressure.
DSO was relatively stable at around 79–80 days from FY2019 to FY2021, before increasing to 87.3 days in FY2022 and 98.2 days in FY2023. FY2022 should be treated cautiously because of the NTT Ltd. consolidation. The higher DSO means more Revenue was tied up in outstanding receivables before being converted into cash.
By contrast, Contract Asset Days remained relatively stable at around 11–15 days, while Inventory remained a small part of the operating cycle. This suggests that the main pressure was not concentrated in unbilled work or Inventory.
NTT DATA also benefited from supplier and customer funding. DPO increased over the period, while Contract Liabilities remained significant, helping offset part of the capital tied up in receivables.

Peer Benchmarking and Core Diagnosis
NTT DATA was compared with Accenture, Capgemini, CGI and TCS to assess whether its working-capital performance differs from peers.
The clearest difference is in DSO. NTT DATA’s clean-period DSO is approximately 80.1 days, compared with a peer median of 57.2 days, creating a gap of 22.9 days. This indicates that NTT DATA carries more Trade Receivables relative to Revenue than its peers.
Other working-capital measures are more favorable. Contract Asset Days are lower than the peer benchmark, while DPO and Contract Liability Days are higher, indicating greater supplier and customer funding. CFO relative to standardized EBITDA is also broadly in line with peers.
Overall, the evidence suggests that NTT DATA’s main working-capital weakness is concentrated in Trade Receivables and DSO rather than across the whole operating cycle. This makes DSO improvement the main focus of the financial opportunity analysis.

DSO Improvement Opportunity
NTT DATA’s clean-period DSO of approximately 80.1 days is around 22.9 days above the peer median of 57.2 days. However, assuming that the full gap can be eliminated would be too aggressive because differences in customer mix, geography, contracts and payment terms may explain part of the difference.

The Base case is used as the main scenario. It assumes that NTT DATA closes 50% of the observed DSO gap, reducing DSO by approximately 11.4 days to 68.7 days.
This represents a meaningful improvement while still leaving NTT DATA’s DSO above the peer median, making it more conservative than assuming full convergence.

Financial Impact
Using normalized FY2025 Revenue of approximately ¥4,875.1 billion, the potential cash release from DSO improvement is calculated as:
Cash Release = Revenue × DSO Improvement ÷ 365 
Under the Base case, an 11.4-day improvement in DSO could release approximately ¥152.7 billion from Trade Receivables.
This represents a one-time balance-sheet cash release, not additional Revenue, EBIT, EBITDA or Net Income.
If the released cash is used to reduce or avoid interest-bearing financing, it could also create an ongoing financing benefit. Using a historical effective financing-cost proxy of approximately 3.84%:
Annual Financing Benefit = Cash Release × Financing Cost
Using a historical effective financing-cost proxy of approximately 3.84%, the Base case implies a potential annual pre-tax financing benefit of approximately ¥5.9 billion under 100% financing utilization.
If only 75% of the released cash is used to reduce or avoid financing:
Realized Financing Benefit = Cash Release × Financing Cost × Financing Utilization
This gives approximately ¥4.4 billion per year.
Overall, improving Trade Receivable efficiency could provide a meaningful liquidity benefit while also reducing financing costs, depending on how the released cash is ultimately used.

Conclusion
The analysis identifies Trade Receivables and DSO as NTT DATA’s clearest working-capital weakness. NTT DATA’s DSO is materially above the peer benchmark, while Contract Asset Days, supplier funding, customer funding and operating cash conversion are comparatively stronger or broadly in line.
Under the Base case, closing 50% of the observed DSO gap would improve DSO by approximately 11.4 days, reducing it from 80.1 days to 68.7 days. At normalized FY2025 Revenue, this could release approximately ¥152.7 billion of cash from Trade Receivables.
If the released cash were fully used to reduce or avoid interest-bearing financing, the potential annual pre-tax financing benefit would be approximately ¥5.9 billion, or around ¥4.4 billion under a 75% financing-utilization assumption.
Overall, the analysis supports a targeted improvement in Trade Receivable efficiency rather than a broad working-capital restructuring.
Limitations
The analysis is subject to several limitations. Peer companies differ in customer mix, geography, contract structure and payment terms, meaning that the full DSO gap may not be operationally removable.
The exact cause of NTT DATA’s higher DSO also cannot be confirmed using public financial statements alone. The 25%, 50% and 75% improvement cases are therefore analytical scenarios rather than forecasts.
FY2022 is treated as a structural-break year because of the consolidation of NTT Ltd. In addition, the 3.84% financing-cost assumption is a historical proxy rather than a guaranteed future financing rate.
Finally, the estimated financing benefit depends on how NTT DATA ultimately uses the released cash.
